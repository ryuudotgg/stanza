title: after-multiline
description: The blank line after a statement that spans lines.
---

<RuleSummary />

A statement is multi-line when it starts and ends on different lines: a call with its arguments on their own lines, an object literal, an `if` whose block body spans lines, a loop, a `try`. A braceless `if`, `for` or `while` whose body sits on the next line counts as one line when its joined line fits the formatter width, since your formatter joins it, so it gets the findings its one-line twin gets. An `if` with `else` counts as one line when every arm fits on its own line, as in `if (ready) send();` over `else close();`. A comment between the header and the body, or a body too wide to join, keeps it multi-line. The rule applies to every gap after such a statement, at every level including module top level, except the gap between a multi-line import and the import or re-export after it.

A too long braceless guard stays on two lines and gets a blank line after it. This rule outranks every join rule, so a multi-line declaration never joins the `if`, `return`, loop or `try` that reads it. The example shows that: the guard reads `raw`, and the blank line still goes in.

When the next statement begins on the line where the multi-line statement ends, nothing is inserted between them. A switch clause whose body starts on the line after its label is multi-line too, so the blank line before the next label is reported under this rule rather than [switch-clauses](/rules/switch-clauses).
